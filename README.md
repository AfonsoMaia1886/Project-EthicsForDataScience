# Ethics for Data Science: The "Light Mirror" Scenario

## 📖 Overview
This presentation explores ethical challenges in Data Science through the 'LUMEN'/'Light Mirror' scenario. It analyzes conflicts between citizens, scientists, companies, and governments, proposing crucial guidelines: banning single-score systems, algorithmic transparency, and protecting vulnerable groups.

## 🎯 Objective
To critically analyze the societal implications of widespread, automated scoring systems (like LUMEN) and to establish a robust ethical framework that protects human rights, autonomy, and fairness in a data-driven world.

## 👥 Stakeholders & Conflicting Interests
The analysis highlights the tension between the varying goals of different stakeholders:
- **Citizens / Data Subjects:** Seek autonomy, privacy, and fair opportunities.
- **Children & Future Generations:** Unable to consent, yet they bear the longest-lasting consequences.
- **Data Scientists & Engineers (e.g., Iris):** Often caught between professional ethics, employment stability, and institutional pressure.
- **Government / Regulators:** Desire efficiency and political legitimacy, but may resist transparency that exposes systemic failures.
- **Private Operators / Shareholders:** Driven by profit-maximization; tend to favor scale and technological lock-in.
- **Insurers, Banks, & Employers:** Attempt to externalize risks and costs onto individuals using data scores.

## 🛡️ Proposed Ethical Guardrails
To prevent harm and ensure responsible AI deployment, the following limits must exist:
1. **No Single-Score Governance:** Decisions regarding education, healthcare, and justice must use domain-specific, auditable models. A universal score concentrates too much power and makes errors catastrophic.
2. **Human-in-the-Loop:** Prohibition of fully automated decisions in high-stakes domains, ensuring meaningful human judgment remains (aligning with the EU AI Act).
3. **Fundamental Data Rights:** Guaranteed right to explanation, contestation, and erasure. Without these, individuals are merely "data objects".
4. **Protection of Vulnerable Groups:** Heightened protection for children and protected categories to prevent early algorithmic labels from becoming lifelong destinies.
5. **Algorithmic Transparency:** Mandatory visibility into training data, objectives, and error rates. You cannot govern what you cannot see.
6. **Public-Interest Red Lines:** Absolute prohibition on the algorithmic scoring of political opinions, sexuality, religion, or reproductive choices, as these are constitutive of democratic personhood.
7. **Continuous Re-evaluation:** Implementation of sunset clauses, recognizing that technologies deemed proportionate today may become disproportionate tomorrow.

## 💡 Conclusion
Borrowing from Fiesler's philosophy: We should innovate like optimists—imagining the technology's genuine ability to improve lives—while preparing like pessimists by designing legal and institutional guardrails *before* deployment, rather than reacting after the harm is done.
